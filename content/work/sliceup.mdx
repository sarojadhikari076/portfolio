Sliceup was company work, so this page sticks to what I can say publicly. The code is private, and the numbers are the ones on my résumé.

## The job

I joined as the first engineer, which meant there was no system to inherit and nobody to hand anything to. Over eighteen months I:

- built the platform from scratch and owned its architecture, infrastructure and delivery;
- designed a five-component system covering the API, the dashboards and real-time order processing;
- built real-time order and driver allocation, which cut delivery latency across the network;
- scaled it to more than 110 vendors and 10,000 users with consistent performance under load;
- made the technical calls across backend, frontend, AWS and AI tooling, working directly with the founders.

## What being first taught me

**Every decision is a decision about the next hire.** A clever abstraction I could hold in my head was a week of onboarding for whoever came next. I learned to prefer the boring option whenever the clever one only saved me time.

**Operations are part of the product.** Vendors don't care whether an order went missing in the API or the dashboard. Owning the whole path meant I could fix a problem where it started instead of where it showed up.

**Saying no is the senior skill.** With founders, the queue of good ideas is always longer than the week. The most useful thing I did, again and again, was turn a request into the smallest change that would tell us whether it mattered.
